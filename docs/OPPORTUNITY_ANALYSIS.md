# Income Project — Opportunity Analysis

Working log of the business search. Records what was evaluated, what was
ruled out, and why — so we never re-litigate a dead hypothesis.

Status: **vertical not yet selected.** Discovery phase.

---

## Founder profile

- FOH Director at a Chick-fil-A, Greensboro NC (Triad)
- ~20+ hrs/week available outside the job
- Comfortable building with AI assistance; design capable
- Willing to do cold/in-person outreach
- **No existing audience or community standing outside CFA**

### Hard constraints

1. **IP.** Employment agreement assigns work-product ideas. Under
   [N.C.G.S. § 66-57.1](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/PDF/BySection/Chapter_66/GS_66-57.1.pdf),
   assignment clauses are unenforceable for inventions made entirely on
   own time and own equipment — **except** inventions that (i) relate to
   the employer's business, or (ii) result from work performed for the
   employer. Restaurant/QSR operations software hits **both** exceptions.
   → **Restaurant and QSR software is off the table.**
2. **No channel.** Access must be built, not assumed.
3. **Time.** ~20 hrs/week; MVP must stay small.

## Ruled out, with evidence

### Restaurant / QSR software — ALL of it
Blocked by the IP constraint above (relates to employer's business AND
results from work performed for employer). Not revisitable while employed
by a CFA Operator.

### CFA team-management / coaching wedge
Three entrenched vendors, one owned by the buyers:
- **[OneClick](https://www.oneclickapp.com/)** — 600+ CFA stores, 70k+ team
  members. [$199 / $349 / $549 per month](https://www.oneclickapp.com/pricing).
- **[Nation](https://www.nationapp.com/)** by **[GIOA](https://gioa.info/)** —
  an association *founded by CFA Operators*. HR, training, employee mgmt.
- **VSBL** — referenced by OneClick's [switch page](https://www.oneclickapp.com/switch).

Useful takeaway retained: CFA Operators demonstrably pay $199–549/mo for
third-party software. High-WTP buyers exist in QSR — we just can't serve them.

## Current strategy: access-first

With no channel and no domain, the vertical is chosen by **what can be
physically entered**, then the problem is discovered inside it. Not the
reverse.

### Primary access hypothesis — High Point Market
[Largest home furnishings trade show in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Point_Market),
~20 minutes from Greensboro:
- ~2,000 exhibitors, ~180 buildings
- ~75,000 attendees; ~40,000 retailers and interior designers
- April and October each year
- **Trade-only** — credentials required (unverified; blocking question)

Known incumbents in the adjacent space: Studio Designer, Design Manager,
Houzz Pro. Going to *find* a problem, not to sell a preconceived one.

### Fallbacks
- Local Triad B2B walk-ins in a dense, moneyed, enumerable vertical
- Cold email to a national enumerable list (scales; lowest trust)

## Next 3 weeks (~60 hrs) — 80% discovery, 20% prep

1. **Week 1 — access.** Confirm High Point Market credentials + exact dates.
   Pick two backup Triad verticals; 10 in-person visits.
2. **Week 2 — volume.** 20 discovery conversations. Never mention software.
3. **Week 3 — synthesis.** Find the pain that *repeats* across 7+ people in
   different companies. Then select, and only then write requirements.

### Discovery questions (non-leading)
1. Walk me through the last time [recurring workflow] went wrong.
2. Where does that get written down, if anywhere?
3. How do you find out about a problem — early or late?
4. What happened the last time you lost a customer or a job unexpectedly?
5. What does your owner/boss ask for that's hardest to produce?
6. What are you doing on paper or in a group chat that you wish you weren't?
7. What software do you pay for right now?
8. Who decides when you buy a new tool, and what made the last one a yes?
9. What did you try that didn't stick, and why did it stop?
10. If you could hand one part of your week to someone else, what would it be?

### Validation thresholds
- **Weak** — polite answers, described workarounds, nothing repeats.
- **Promising** — same specific pain unprompted in 5+ of 10; "tell me when
  you build that."
- **Strong** — someone says they'd pay, or commits to a paid pilot.

## Honest expectations

With a warm channel, ~24 customers at $129/mo was realistic. Without one,
first paying stranger is **3–6 months out**, not 6 weeks. The dominant risk
is not picking wrong — it's quitting in month four before the channel
compounds.
