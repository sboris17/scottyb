# Scripts

Ten scripts, produced with the prompt in [`../prompts/script.md`](../prompts/script.md).

One niche: how the creator business actually works. Each stands alone, but they
compound in order — batch 1 sets up the problems, batch 2 explains the machinery
underneath them, batch 3 applies both to specific structures. Several arrive at
the same conclusion from different directions, which is the spine of the series.

## Publish order

1. `sidemen-group-moat` — the recognisable name does the discovery work.
2. `your-niche-is-your-salary` — most immediately useful, best retention.
3. `the-content-house-always-collapses` — sequel to #1, audience arrives holding half the argument.
4. `the-click-ate-the-content` — fully evergreen, nothing to decay.
5. `the-clip-economy` — pairs with #4: the sentence you don't control.
6. `the-access-business` — the deeper cut for people who came back.
7. `you-dont-own-them` — needs #1 already up, or it ends on a diagnosis with no treatment.
8. `why-everyone-started-a-podcast` — payoff episode, reuses machinery from #2, #6 and #3.
9. `when-content-costs-nothing` — most time-sensitive, so don't sit on it. See its batch note.
10. `the-back-catalogue-doesnt-pay` — last, because it makes the other nine look like they were building toward something.

## What's in each file

Narration of roughly 1,430–1,500 words (~10 minutes at 150 wpm), a loop map with
timestamps, eight ranked title options, a thumbnail direction, and a **Before you
record** section listing every claim to verify.

## House rules

- **No invented specifics.** No numbers, dates, counts, RPMs, listener figures, or
  valuation multiples unless verified from a primary source. This niche
  fact-checks in the comments, and being confidently wrong once costs more than
  the video earned.
- **No named individuals as cautionary tales.** Structural arguments don't need a
  villain, and a real person's burnout, ban, bad clip, or collapsed house is not
  b-roll. This binds hardest on the content house and clip scripts, where the real
  examples carry disputed allegations.
- **Arguments stay labelled as arguments.** Where a script is reasoning rather
  than reporting, the notes say so and the narration is phrased to read that way
  out loud.
- **Narration only in the script body.** No shot lists or stage directions; that
  lives in the notes underneath.
